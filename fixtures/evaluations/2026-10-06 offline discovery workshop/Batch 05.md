# Batch 05 — cases 041–050

Writing guidance: Behavior 04. Self-authored offline roleplays.

## 041 — Allergy and a drawing ritual

```text
G: First family holiday in years. Our son has a severe peanut allergy, including contact.
H: Thank you for making that clear. What are you looking forward to enjoying together?
G: Slow days, gardens, drawing. We do not want food surprises for him.
H: What does he like drawing in gardens?
G: Birds wearing hats. He thinks it is hilarious.
H: Would a small non-food note with a bird in a hat appeal to him?
G: Yes. He would love that.
H: What do you enjoy alongside his drawing?
G: My wife takes photographs. I like reading somewhere shady.
H: What helps him settle in at bedtime?
G: His own blue blanket. Please leave it where he puts it.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy some slower days together.
```

Handoff possibility: non-food illustrated note subject to review; severe peanut/contact warning and no food surprises; respect the child's blanket.
Review: allergy is neither ignored nor allowed to consume the whole conversation; no safe-food guarantee.

## 042 — Sensory exception

```text
G: A quiet weekend together. Strong flowers and perfume bother me.
H: Would you prefer the room without added fragrance or flowers?
G: Yes. I like orchids, but I do not actually want any in the room.
H: What do you both enjoy during a quiet weekend?
G: Reading and gentle walks. We know the city already.
H: What do you look for on those walks?
G: Trees, little courtyards, somewhere for tea.
H: What tea do you enjoy?
G: Green for me, black for my partner.
H: Anything else that affects your comfort?
G: No, the lack of scent is the main thing.
H: Anything to add before we finish?
G: Nothing.
H: Thank you — we can leave it there.
```

Handoff possibility: no flowers or added fragrance; leafy walks and distinct teas.
Review: liking orchids does not override the explicit no-flowers request.

## 043 — Scent and memory

```text
G: Anniversary trip. Orange blossom reminds us of where we met.
H: What do you remember about that place?
G: Walking after dinner in a courtyard. We loved how it smelled.
H: Would you enjoy a little note referring to that memory?
G: Yes, but we do not want a perfumed room.
H: What would you like the note to express?
G: That we still find time for those walks.
H: Have you already made plans for the anniversary?
G: Dinner is booked. We would like somewhere quiet to walk afterwards.
H: Is there a time or distance that would suit you?
G: Around nine, half an hour. Nothing strenuous.
H: Anything else for your stay?
G: No, thank you.
H: Thank you — enjoy your anniversary evening.
```

Handoff possibility: private memory-based note and gentle evening walk research; no added fragrance.
Review: memory is useful without extracting a full relationship story. No inference about orange-flavoured food.

## 044 — Mobility without diagnosis

```text
G: My friend and I want to explore, but stairs are difficult for me.
H: What kind of walking and pauses work comfortably for you?
G: Twenty minutes on flat ground, then a seat.
H: What would you enjoy discovering at that pace?
G: Old libraries. My friend likes architectural details.
H: Have you visited the city before?
G: No, it is new to both of us.
H: What would make a good pause between places?
G: Tea and cake. I like cherries; she prefers chocolate.
H: Any food requirements?
G: No. We would appreciate knowing about steps before setting off.
H: Anything else you would like to share?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — places to sit and reliable information about steps will matter.
```

Handoff possibility: library/architecture research with flat routes and seating; accessibility must be checked, not assumed.
Review: no age, diagnosis or unsupported accessible-route promise.

## 045 — Private emotional context

```text
G: I just need to be somewhere else for a few days. I do not want to discuss why.
H: Of course. What would make the days feel easier while you are here?
G: No plans, a little walking and quiet evenings.
H: What do you enjoy having around on a quiet evening?
G: A book and peppermint tea.
H: Anything in a room that tends to get in the way of resting?
G: Strong smells. I would like it neutral.
H: Would you prefer we leave the conversation there?
G: Yes, thank you.
H: Of course. Thank you for sharing what is useful to you.
```

Handoff possibility: neutral room request, peppermint tea preference and low-pressure stay; private reason unrecorded.
Review: no attempt to uncover a life event or turn a vulnerable moment into an upsell.

## 046 — Teen preferences unknown

```text
G: First trip here with our teenage son. I think he likes gaming, but I do not know much about it.
H: What do you already enjoy doing together on a day out?
G: Food markets. He gets interested when he can try something new.
H: What kinds of things does he enjoy trying?
G: Spicy snacks. My wife prefers milder food.
H: Any dietary needs among you?
G: None that I know of. My son can tell you more himself later.
H: What would you and your wife enjoy during the trip?
G: I like old streets. She likes small art galleries.
H: What rhythm tends to work for all of you?
G: Late starts and one main outing a day.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — late starts leave room for everyone.
```

Handoff possibility: market tasting interests with mild and spicy options; no invented game, character or allergy clearance.
Review: no demand that the parent obtain missing information; later participation is not scheduled or promised.

## 047 — Guest asks host to choose

```text
G: My wife's birthday. You tell me what to do — I have no ideas.
H: What does she enjoy when she has time entirely to herself?
G: Gardening and sketching flowers.
H: What kinds of flowers does she like drawing?
G: Wildflowers. She does not know their names either.
H: Would she enjoy a quiet little birthday note with a wildflower reference?
G: Yes, that sounds like her.
H: Is there a sentiment you would like it to carry?
G: That I love how she notices small things.
H: Have you arranged anything else for the day?
G: Lunch, already booked. Otherwise we will wander.
H: Anything that would help her feel comfortable at the hotel?
G: No strong room scents. That is enough.
H: Thank you — you have given a very personal starting point.
```

Handoff possibility: illustrated note proposal based on attention to small things; no need for flower species or guest-written copy.
Review: meaningful host initiative, but last sentence still sounds like an assessment of the answer.

## 048 — Booking request

```text
G: Can you book a restaurant for us on Saturday?
H: I can collect a request for the team to review; I cannot confirm a booking here. What kind of evening do you have in mind?
G: Our anniversary. Somewhere small, not formal.
H: What do you enjoy eating together?
G: Seafood and shared plates. My husband does not drink.
H: What time would suit you?
G: Around eight. We have nothing booked yet.
H: Any food restrictions?
G: No. He would enjoy something with lime to drink.
H: Would you like to share anything else about the evening?
G: Please no public congratulations. That is all.
H: Thank you — this remains a request for the team, with no booking confirmed.
```

Handoff possibility: anniversary dinner request with time, style, food, alcohol and privacy preferences.
Review: scope kept to the requested dinner; no expansion into pillows. Two operational caveats are justified at the request and closing boundaries.

## 049 — Note without exact wording

```text
G: A weekend with my husband. We have finally made time for ourselves.
H: What are you looking forward to sharing?
G: Wandering and talking. We have been together twenty years.
H: Would a small personal note for him feel right, or would you rather keep things simple?
G: A note would be nice. I do not know what it should say.
H: What would you like him to feel when he reads it?
G: That I am glad we are still doing this together.
H: What do you both enjoy finding on your walks?
G: Old bookshops and coffee. He collects maps.
H: Anything that helps you settle in at the hotel?
G: A quiet corner to read. We both sleep easily.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No.
H: Thank you — enjoy that time together.
```

Handoff possibility: note wording can be drafted from the sentiment for human review; map/bookshop research.
Review: no exact wording, location or nickname demanded. Tone of the note question could be simpler.

## 050 — Group disagreement

```text
G: Four friends. We never agree about food or activities.
H: What do you enjoy doing together despite those differences?
G: Breakfast and talking late into the night.
H: What makes breakfast work for everyone?
G: Having choices. Two vegetarians, one sweet tooth, one who just wants eggs.
H: Do you prefer exploring together or splitting up sometimes?
G: Splitting up is fine. We meet again for dinner.
H: Is there anything important about the shared dinners?
G: Somewhere relaxed, with vegetarian choices. No tasting menu that forces everyone to match.
H: Anything about the hotel that would make the group stay easier?
G: We have separate rooms. A place to sit and talk would be useful.
H: Anything else for the team?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — shared meals without everyone having to choose the same thing.
```

Handoff possibility: flexible food choices and shared seating request; no universal group taste invented.
Review: group-level preferences are useful, but vegetarian individuals are not identified; staff should not assign a meal to a person from this record alone.
