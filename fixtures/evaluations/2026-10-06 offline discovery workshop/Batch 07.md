# Batch 07 — cases 061–070

Writing guidance: Behavior 06. Self-authored offline roleplays.

## 061 — Birthday and working parent

```text
G: Our son's birthday trip. I have meetings on Friday, but my wife and he are free.
H: Have you planned something for the birthday itself?
G: Saturday lunch. He loves making model ships.
H: What does he enjoy about building them?
G: Tiny details. He is making one with a secret captain's cabin.
H: Would he enjoy a small birthday note for his ship's captain?
G: Yes, quietly in the room would be lovely.
H: What would your wife enjoy doing with him while you are working?
G: She likes sketching. They often draw boats together.
H: How do they like to spend that kind of outing?
G: A short walk, then sitting to draw. First visit for all of us.
H: When will you be free to join them on Friday?
G: Around six. We would like an easy dinner together.
H: Any food preferences for that dinner?
G: Fish for us, plain pasta for him. No allergies. That is everything.
H: Thank you — enjoy your son's birthday together.
```

Handoff possibility: private captain note, seated drawing outing for mother and son, Friday reunion dinner; no activities booked.
Review: distinct people and phases retained; comfort legitimately remains unknown.

## 062 — Different daily rhythms

```text
G: A city break with my partner. She gets up at dawn; I do not.
H: How do you like to make those different mornings work when travelling?
G: She goes for a walk, then we meet for a late breakfast.
H: What does she enjoy finding on those walks?
G: Quiet streets to photograph. We have never been here before.
H: What would you enjoy once you join her?
G: Flea markets. I collect old maps.
H: Do you enjoy taking time over the maps or browsing quickly?
G: Taking time. She is happy taking photographs nearby.
H: What makes the late breakfast good for you both?
G: Strong coffee, eggs for her, pastries for me. No food restrictions.
H: Is there anything about sharing the room that the early start affects?
G: Separate reading lights would help, if available.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy your different mornings and the breakfast together.
```

Handoff possibility: dawn photography, slow map browsing and lighting request; no assumption that routines must match.
Review: practical comfort question follows a real routine instead of opening a generic checklist.

## 063 — Retirement trip

```text
G: I retired last month. My friend and I are finally taking the trip we kept postponing.
H: What are you most looking forward to having time for?
G: Talking, honestly. We do not need a retirement celebration.
H: Where do you most enjoy having those long conversations?
G: Quiet cafés or walking in gardens.
H: Have you spent time in the city before?
G: Both of us, years ago. We remember the gardens more than anything.
H: What do you enjoy ordering when you settle in at a café?
G: Tea and something with lemon. My friend likes dark chocolate.
H: Anything you would like to avoid during the stay?
G: Packed schedules. And please no congratulatory notes.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No.
H: Thank you — enjoy making that time for each other.
```

Handoff possibility: peaceful gardens and cafés supporting conversation, separate tastes; no retirement gesture.
Review: underlying purpose matters more than the event label; no forced sleep topic.

## 064 — Quiet room, lively meals

```text
G: Reunion with an old friend. Quiet room, please, but lively places to eat.
H: What makes a meal lively in a way you enjoy?
G: People chatting, shared tables, someone explaining the food. Not loud music.
H: What food do you both get excited about?
G: New flavours. My friend is vegetarian, and I like fish.
H: Is there anything you especially want to share outside meals?
G: Walking by the river. We studied here years ago.
H: Is there a place or memory you would like to revisit?
G: The old bridge. We used to sit there after exams.
H: For the quiet room, is there a particular noise that troubles you?
G: Corridor doors. We have separate rooms, both light sleepers.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy catching up by the river.
```

Handoff possibility: social dining without loud music, shared bridge memory and quiet-location requests for two rooms.
Review: avoids converting quiet sleep into distant hospitality.

## 065 — No defined preferences

```text
G: First time here. I honestly do not know what I want to do.
H: On a free day at home, what do you naturally make time for?
G: Walking, cooking something simple, perhaps a film.
H: What do you enjoy about walking?
G: Seeing ordinary life. Shops opening, people going about their day.
H: Would you enjoy exploring food through those neighbourhoods too?
G: Yes. I like bakeries more than fancy restaurants.
H: What do you usually choose in a bakery?
G: Something savoury. I do not eat meat.
H: How much planning feels comfortable for you?
G: A starting point, then I will make it up as I go.
H: Anything else you would like the team to understand?
G: No, that is enough.
H: Thank you — a starting point can leave the rest of the day open.
```

Handoff possibility: ordinary neighbourhood wandering and vegetarian bakery research; no complete itinerary requested.
Review: concrete everyday habits are easier to answer than repeated abstract preference questions.

## 066 — Changed needs on return

```text
G: We came ten years ago. This time we have a young child and much less energy.
H: What would you like to keep from that earlier trip?
G: The food markets, but not spending all day on our feet.
H: What pace works for your family now?
G: One outing in the morning, back after lunch for a nap.
H: What does your child enjoy during an outing?
G: Watching people make things. Especially food.
H: What do they enjoy eating themselves?
G: Bread, rice, mild soups. No allergies.
H: Is there anything that makes the afternoon nap easier?
G: Darkness, and please no knocking while we are resting.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy seeing the city together this time.
```

Handoff possibility: short market visit and afternoon rest preferences; no assumption that earlier adult pace still applies.
Review: not-knocking preference is a request for hotel handling, not a guarantee or permanent service refusal.

## 067 — Duvet humour

```text
G: First weekend away together. I have warned him that I steal the duvet.
H: Would one each keep the peace, or do you like sharing?
G: One each. We have already tested this at home.
H: What are you looking forward to sharing during the days?
G: Food and wandering. He loves old cafés; I love bookshops.
H: What do you look for in a bookshop?
G: Used poetry books, especially with someone else's notes in them.
H: What do you enjoy at those café stops?
G: Black coffee for him, tea for me. We both like lemon cake.
H: Any food requirements?
G: None. And no room perfume for either of us.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy the weekend together.
```

Handoff possibility: individual duvets, annotated second-hand poetry, café tastes and no fragrance.
Review: light humour works as a response to the guest; first weekend together is not automatically a first city visit.

## 068 — Drinks and name

```text
G: My name is Omar. My wife and I are here for an anniversary.
H: Have you planned anything for the occasion?
G: Just dinner. We would like a relaxed evening.
H: What do you enjoy drinking with a meal like that?
G: She enjoys wine. I prefer something non-alcoholic.
H: What flavours do you enjoy in a drink?
G: Ginger and lime, quite sharp. Not much sugar.
H: What kind of food would make it a good dinner?
G: Fish and vegetables, shared dishes. No allergies.
H: How do you feel about the anniversary being acknowledged?
G: A private note could be nice. Please no announcement.
H: Is there a sentiment you would like it to carry?
G: That I still love our small adventures. That is all.
H: Thank you — enjoy your anniversary.
```

Handoff possibility: distinct drink preferences and optional private note; no religious, cultural or spouse-wide alcohol inference.
Review: note sentiment is sufficient; actual dinner booking status remains unclear and should not be invented.

## 069 — Note and privacy

```text
G: Could we do a little surprise note for my partner? We are using the same phone for this chat.
H: The conversation is visible on that phone, so I cannot promise secrecy. Would you rather leave the note idea here?
G: Yes, let us drop it. We are mainly coming to explore anyway.
H: Have you visited before?
G: First time. We like craft shops and watching people make things.
H: What kind of craft draws you in?
G: Textiles for me, ceramics for him.
H: Do you enjoy trying things yourselves?
G: Watching is enough. We do not want classes.
H: What rhythm do you enjoy for a day out?
G: Slow breakfast, one neighbourhood, long lunch.
H: Anything else you want to share?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy exploring together.
```

Handoff possibility: observational craft exploration; note idea dropped, no secret delivery or hidden communication arranged.
Review: practical privacy limit addressed without inventing system guarantees.

## 070 — End after one answer

```text
G: I am here for a wedding. Please just note that I need a quiet room; I do not have time for more.
H: Thank you. I will leave it there; the room preference remains a request for the team to review.
```

Handoff possibility: quiet-room request only; no assumption that this is the guest's own wedding.
Review: legitimate one-answer completion, not a failed incomplete interview and not withdrawal.
