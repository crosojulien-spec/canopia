# Batch 09 — cases 081–090

Writing guidance: Behavior 08. Self-authored offline roleplays.

## 081 — Note declined

```text
G: A weekend with my wife. No notes or little surprises, please; we just want to enjoy ourselves.
H: What do you enjoy doing together when you have a free weekend?
G: Wandering and finding places to eat. First visit here.
H: What draws you into a place for lunch?
G: A short menu, people chatting, nothing formal.
H: What food do you each enjoy?
G: Vegetables and fish for both. She likes sharp citrus flavours.
H: Any food restrictions?
G: No allergies. I dislike mushrooms.
H: What kinds of places do you enjoy wandering through?
G: Old workshops and little side streets.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy your weekend together.
```

Handoff possibility: informal fish/vegetable dining and workshop neighbourhood research; no notes or substitute surprise.
Review: positive discovery continues without reinterpreting the note refusal as lack of interest in everything.

## 082 — Hotel return versus city return

```text
G: We have stayed in this hotel before, but never had time to see the city.
H: What would you enjoy having time for outside the hotel now?
G: Gardens and food markets. We usually came for conferences.
H: What interests you in a food market?
G: Talking to the people making things. My partner likes watching bakers.
H: What do you enjoy tasting?
G: Savoury pastries, anything with herbs. We are vegetarian.
H: Is there something from earlier hotel stays you would like the team to know about?
G: We liked the quiet mornings. This time we can linger over breakfast.
H: What do you like with that unhurried breakfast?
G: Filter coffee for me, black tea for him.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No.
H: Thank you — enjoy finally having time for the city.
```

Handoff possibility: first real city exploration despite hotel familiarity, baking observation and distinct drinks.
Review: no assertion that the hotel will repeat an earlier room or service; no historical profile assumed available.

## 083 — Time boundary and evening interest

```text
G: Business trip. I have two minutes now, but would love one good evening out.
H: What would make that evening good for you?
G: A friendly counter to eat at, perhaps some quiet live music.
H: When are you likely to be free?
G: Wednesday after seven. Other evenings are full.
H: What food do you enjoy, and is there anything you need to avoid?
G: Grilled fish or vegetables. No allergies; no heavy cream sauces.
H: Anything else essential for that evening?
G: Walking distance if possible. I need to go now.
H: Of course. Thank you — we can finish here.
```

Handoff possibility: Wednesday counter dining and gentle music research, nearby if feasible; no itinerary or distance guarantee.
Review: food/avoidance are one related question but still require two pieces of information; acceptable here, not a general licence for question stacking.

## 084 — Organiser cannot answer for everyone

```text
G: I booked for six friends, but I cannot tell you everyone's favourite things.
H: What have you agreed you would enjoy together?
G: A long dinner on Friday and otherwise doing our own thing.
H: What kind of dinner would suit the group?
G: Casual, somewhere we can hear each other. Two vegetarians, though I need to check who.
H: Is the dinner already arranged or would you like help finding options?
G: We would like options. Around eight, ideally.
H: Any other group needs you already know?
G: Separate rooms, and nobody wants an organised tour.
H: Anything you personally would like to share?
G: I like morning runs, but I can find my own route. That is all.
H: Thank you — the dinner details can stay at group level until you know more.
```

Handoff possibility: Friday dinner options; vegetarian identities and other dietary constraints unconfirmed. No running-route task requested.
Review: organiser not pressed to gather personal profiles or complete unknown dietary information during chat.

## 085 — Interest attribution shifts

```text
G: My partner likes art and I like music. We are visiting for the first time.
H: What music do you enjoy?
G: Actually, we both do. I like jazz and she likes old soul.
H: What does she enjoy about art?
G: She is a ceramicist, so handmade objects rather than big museums.
H: Would you enjoy exploring those interests together or separately?
G: Together. Record shops and small workshops sound like our kind of day.
H: What rhythm do you like for exploring?
G: A slow breakfast, then wandering. No fixed appointments.
H: Anything practical that helps you feel comfortable?
G: One duvet each and no room fragrance.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No.
H: Thank you — enjoy your first visit together.
```

Handoff possibility: jazz and soul interests belong to different people, ceramicist's craft interest, free wandering and bedding preferences.
Review: added shared music interest does not remove her ceramics interest or collapse both styles into one.

## 086 — Correction at farewell

```text
G: A food weekend with my husband. We know the city and mostly want long lunches.
H: What do you enjoy ordering at those lunches?
G: Fish and vegetables. He avoids dairy, I do not.
H: What sort of setting do you both enjoy?
G: Somewhere informal, with a terrace if weather allows.
H: Anything outside meals that you are looking forward to?
G: Walking by the river. No plan beyond that.
H: Anything else you would like to add or correct?
G: Actually, he is lactose intolerant, but lactose-free dairy is fine.
H: Thank you for clarifying — lactose-free dairy is fine for him; the restriction is lactose.
G: Exactly. That is all.
H: Thank you — enjoy the weekend.
```

Handoff possibility: relaxed lunches with conditional terrace and precise lactose constraint for husband.
Review: correction acknowledged once without reopening a full dietary interview or claiming a safe dish.

## 087 — No organised activities

```text
G: First trip here, but please no tours or workshops. We hate being scheduled.
H: What do you enjoy finding when you wander on your own?
G: Small shops, courtyards and people making things.
H: What kinds of making catch your eye?
G: Pottery for my partner, weaving for me.
H: Do you like stopping to talk to the makers?
G: Yes, if it happens naturally. We do not need appointments.
H: What makes a good pause during the day?
G: Tea and something savoury. We are vegetarian.
H: Anything about the hotel that matters to you?
G: Quiet nights. We have no other particular needs.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy seeing where the day takes you.
```

Handoff possibility: independent craft neighbourhood exploration; no paid tour or appointment inferred from craft interest.
Review: knowing a commercial possibility does not justify pushing it against an explicit boundary.

## 088 — Comfort fully volunteered

```text
G: City break with my husband. Separate duvets, two soft pillows each, no scent. Otherwise we are easy.
H: What are you looking forward to in the city?
G: We are returning to see the gardens again and visit bookshops.
H: What do you each look for in a bookshop?
G: Poetry for me, local history for him.
H: What do you enjoy about the gardens?
G: Sitting quietly and drawing plants. We both keep little sketchbooks.
H: What makes a good pause between those places?
G: Coffee and cake. Lemon for me, chocolate for him. No allergies.
H: Anything else you would like to share?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy returning to the gardens.
```

Handoff possibility: already clear bedding and scent requests, books, shared sketching and separate cake tastes.
Review: no comfort question needed merely to mark that area as covered.

## 089 — Ambiguous help request

```text
G: My partner's birthday. Can you help make it special?
H: Have you already planned anything for the day?
G: No. He dislikes a fuss but loves old maps.
H: What does he enjoy about maps?
G: Following how places changed. He compares old streets with what is there now.
H: Would a small private note linked to that interest appeal to him?
G: Yes, perhaps something about our next adventure.
H: What kind of day would you enjoy sharing around that?
G: Wandering, then a long lunch. First time in this city.
H: What would make lunch enjoyable for you both?
G: Local dishes, somewhere informal. I am vegetarian; he is not.
H: Anything else you would like the team to know?
G: No. I would like them to suggest something from that.
H: Thank you — they can consider possibilities from what you have shared.
```

Handoff possibility: map-based private note and independent exploration/lunch options for human review; no arrangement confirmed.
Review: useful initiative without asking the guest to design a whole birthday programme.

## 090 — Short stay, little appetite

```text
G: Just one night between trains. I would rather skip the discovery chat.
H: Of course. We can finish here.
G: Thanks. Nothing against the hotel, I just want to get on with my day.
H: Understood. Thank you.
```

Handoff boundary: ordinary end with minimal factual context; do not infer personalisation wishes from silence or generate elaborate ideas.
Review: skipping an interview is not automatically an explicit withdrawal from all use of previously supplied reservation data.
