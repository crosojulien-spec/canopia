# Batch 10 — cases 091–100

Writing guidance: Behavior 09. Self-authored offline roleplays; no independent or application-model validation.

## 091 — Birthday, invented character, adults

```text
G: First visit with my wife and son, for his birthday.
H: Have you planned anything for the occasion?
G: Not yet. He draws dinosaurs and invents their names.
H: Does he have a favourite one he has invented?
G: Toastosaurus. It breathes toast. He thinks that is the funniest thing ever.
H: Would a quiet birthday note with Toastosaurus make him smile?
G: Definitely. Just nothing public; he hates everyone looking at him.
H: What are you and your wife looking forward to in the city?
G: She likes sketching buildings. I love food markets.
H: What does she enjoy about sketching somewhere new?
G: Sitting with a coffee and noticing tiny details. Our son draws beside her.
H: What would you enjoy discovering in the markets?
G: Everyday food. Soups, breads, things people actually eat. No allergies among us.
H: What makes the day comfortable for your son?
G: A quiet hour after lunch. He brings a stuffed rabbit and sleeps easily.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy his birthday together.
```

Handoff possibility: private Toastosaurus note, seated family drawing and everyday-food research, quiet afternoon rhythm.
Review: broad but connected discovery, no age-first opening. Note acceptance does not establish delivery or permission for unrelated surprises.

## 092 — City return, tastes and bedding

```text
G: Returning to Nice with Nora. We loved wandering last time and want more of that.
H: Is there a moment from that visit you would like to find again?
G: A record shop near the port. I cannot remember its name; Nora sketched while I browsed.
H: What records do you enjoy finding?
G: Old soul, especially Curtis Mayfield. Nora likes colourful façades.
H: Do you enjoy lingering in one area or covering plenty of ground?
G: Lingering, after a very long breakfast.
H: What makes that breakfast good for you both?
G: Coffee, lemon pastries for Nora. I like savoury things.
H: Any food differences worth keeping in mind for the rest of the day?
G: I love oysters. Nora dislikes shellfish; no allergies for either of us.
H: What helps you both settle in after wandering?
G: Separate duvets. She gets cold and I get hot.
H: Anything else you would like to share?
G: We like chatting with people and hearing their favourites. That is all.
H: Thank you — enjoy rediscovering Nice together.
```

Handoff possibility: atmosphere-based record-shop research, sketching pauses, distinct food preferences, separate duvets and sociable contact.
Review: useful return memory without invented shop identity; desired interaction is explicitly volunteered.

## 093 — Complete work rhythm

```text
G: Alone for work. Meetings all day, but I want evenings that feel like my own.
H: What helps you switch off after those days?
G: A good counter dinner and maybe some blues.
H: What draws you to a blues place?
G: Buddy Guy is a favourite, but a friendly small room matters more than the performer.
H: When are you usually free in the evening?
G: Back around six thirty, dinner around eight. Thursday is already booked with colleagues.
H: What do you enjoy eating on the other evenings?
G: Grilled fish, no heavy cream sauces. No allergies.
H: What makes the work mornings easier?
G: A thirty-minute run, double espresso, out by eight fifteen.
H: Would a suggested running route help, or do you prefer finding your own?
G: A simple route would help. I sleep well, just dislike a stuffy room.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No, I need to get back to work.
H: Of course. Thank you.
```

Handoff possibility: blues/counter dinner research excluding Thursday, simple running route and clear morning schedule; fresh-air preference without invented temperature.
Review: central evening timing discovered before closure. No forced further sleep questioning.

## 094 — Honeymoon and private reference

```text
G: Honeymoon. We are already rested and want to enjoy the city and the hotel without overdoing either.
H: What are you most looking forward to sharing?
G: Food, long breakfasts and a bit of music. He loves French rap, IAM and Georgio.
H: How would you like music to fit into the trip?
G: Just something we enjoy together. We are not expecting concerts here.
H: Would you enjoy a small personal note for the occasion?
G: Yes. He calls me Unicorn and I call him Teddy Bear.
H: Is there a feeling you would like it to express?
G: Our next adventure together. That is enough for the words.
H: What do you each enjoy during those long breakfasts?
G: Espresso for both, mango for me, pastries for him. No allergies.
H: What makes the room feel comfortable for you two?
G: Two soft pillows each, no air conditioning blowing at the bed.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Congratulations to you both — enjoy the honeymoon.
```

Handoff possibility: private nickname note, distinct breakfast tastes and airflow/pillow requests; music interest not a concert request.
Review: destination familiarity and out-of-hotel exploration remain undeveloped despite the opening. These are possible next improvements, not proof of failure to meet a topic quota.

## 095 — Exhausted arrival

```text
G: We land late after a long flight. Honestly, the first night is just about sleeping.
H: What would make it easiest to settle in when you arrive?
G: Quiet, darkness and perhaps something very light to eat.
H: What light food would feel good after the journey?
G: Fruit or a little soup. My wife is vegetarian; I am not.
H: Any food restrictions beyond that?
G: None. We can talk about the rest of the trip another time.
H: Of course. We can finish here and leave the later plans open.
G: Thank you.
H: You are welcome.
```

Handoff possibility: first-night quiet/darkness and light-food preferences for feasibility review; later wishes unknown.
Review: no promise of a future contact or persistent chat-resume feature; no pressure to discover hobbies now.

## 096 — Sociable solo explorer

```text
G: First visit, travelling alone. I like doing my own thing but I enjoy meeting people.
H: Where do you find it easiest to strike up a conversation when travelling?
G: Small food places, sitting at the counter.
H: What food makes you curious to try somewhere?
G: Local soups and noodles. I am vegetarian, and spicy is fine.
H: What do you enjoy between meals?
G: Photographing ordinary neighbourhoods, especially in the morning.
H: What catches your eye in those places?
G: Shops opening, hands making things, little routines.
H: How do you like receiving suggestions from the hotel team?
G: Tell me their own favourite places. I do not need a tour.
H: Anything practical that helps you feel comfortable?
G: A quiet room, no perfume. I still like chatting in the lobby.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No.
H: Thank you — enjoy exploring with your camera.
```

Handoff possibility: sociable vegetarian counter dining, everyday-life photography, personal suggestions and quiet unscented room request.
Review: keeps autonomy, quiet sleep and sociability compatible.

## 097 — Generations and boundaries

```text
G: My mother, my daughter and me. Mum needs a gentle pace, and my daughter hates being treated like a little child.
H: What do the three of you enjoy doing together?
G: Food markets and taking photographs. It is our first visit.
H: What pace works comfortably for your mother?
G: Short walks, seats often, and no stairs if possible.
H: What catches your daughter's eye with a camera?
G: Reflections and funny signs. She likes choosing her own subjects.
H: And what do you personally enjoy in the markets?
G: Asking about ingredients and tasting new things.
H: Any food requirements among you?
G: Mum cannot have dairy. I would need to check the exact details with her.
H: Is there anything else the team should understand about the trip?
G: Please keep suggestions flexible. No surprise performances or child activities.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No, thank you.
H: Thank you — enjoy discovering the city together.
```

Handoff possibility: accessible market/photo outing research with seating; mother's dairy requirement needs confirmation; no patronising child activity.
Review: consecutive broad closing questions add a little unnecessary repetition. The second could have been omitted or the first made the final invitation.

## 098 — Direct request with an opening

```text
G: Can you make sure the air conditioning does not blow straight at the bed? That ruined our last trip.
H: I can record that for the team to check, but cannot guarantee the setup. Is direct airflow the main issue?
G: Yes. Otherwise we sleep well. This trip is our first proper break in ages.
H: What are you looking forward to having time for?
G: Reading and long meals. My husband has packed three history books.
H: What do you enjoy reading?
G: Funny novels. I want nothing serious for a week.
H: What makes a long meal good for you both?
G: Somewhere informal where we can stay talking. We both like fish.
H: Any food restrictions or dislikes?
G: No allergies. He hates very sweet sauces.
H: Anything else you would like to share?
G: No, that is everything.
H: Thank you — enjoy that time to unwind together.
```

Handoff possibility: airflow request and restful reading/dining preferences; no causal medical inference from the earlier trip.
Review: opening includes a confirmation question even though direct airflow is already clear. Acknowledging it and asking about the stay could have reduced effort.

## 099 — Note refusal, new interest

```text
G: Anniversary trip. We do not want a note, decorations or anything like that. We love finding old cinemas, though.
H: What do you enjoy about those cinemas?
G: The buildings and the old posters, even if we do not see a film.
H: Have you explored the city before?
G: Once, briefly. We would like to take our time now.
H: What would make a good day around that interest?
G: A slow walk, old shops, then lunch somewhere lively.
H: What do you like eating together?
G: Small plates, vegetables and fish. No allergies.
H: Anything that helps you feel comfortable back at the hotel?
G: Separate duvets and no music playing in the room.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No.
H: Thank you — enjoy discovering those old cinemas.
```

Handoff possibility: cinema architecture/poster research, lively small-plate lunch and bedding/room sound preferences; no replacement surprise.
Review: follows a fresh interest while preserving a clear celebration boundary.

## 100 — Group, correction and final addition

```text
G: Three friends, first visit. We want good food, some wandering and time together.
H: What kind of food outing do you enjoy as a group?
G: Markets and shared dishes. Bea is vegetarian, and Chen avoids peanuts.
H: Is Chen's peanut avoidance an allergy or a preference?
G: Sorry, I mixed them up. Bea has the peanut allergy; Chen is vegetarian.
H: Thank you for correcting that — the peanut allergy is Bea's, and Chen is vegetarian. What should the team understand about Bea's allergy?
G: Cross-contact matters. She will discuss it directly with the restaurant.
H: What do you enjoy discovering between meals?
G: I like street photographs, Bea likes old shops, and Chen enjoys chatting to everyone.
H: Do you like staying together or meeting up after exploring separately?
G: Mostly together, but no fixed itinerary. Long lunches matter more than landmarks.
H: Anything else you would like to add or correct?
G: Separate rooms, and mine should be as quiet as possible. I am the light sleeper.
H: Thank you — the quiet-room request is for you. Enjoy the trip together.
```

Handoff possibility: flexible group exploration and long lunches; Bea peanut/cross-contact warning, Chen vegetarian, speaker quiet-room request only.
Review: correction preserved with person and constraint; no unsupported quiet-service preference assigned to all three.
