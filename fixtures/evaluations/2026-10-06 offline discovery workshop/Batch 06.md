# Batch 06 — cases 051–060

Writing guidance: Behavior 05. Self-authored offline roleplays.

## 051 — Short, rich answers

```text
G: First visit. With my wife.
H: What are you hoping to enjoy here?
G: Old shops. Music.
H: What kind of music draws you in?
G: Soul. Curtis Mayfield.
H: What does your wife enjoy finding in old shops?
G: Buttons. She sews.
H: Does she collect them or use them in her sewing?
G: Uses them. Coats mostly.
H: How do you like a day of exploring to start?
G: Long breakfast. Strong coffee.
H: Any food likes or requirements for either of you?
G: Lemon pastries. No allergies.
H: Anything that helps you both sleep well?
G: One duvet each.
H: Anything else to add?
G: Done, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy your first visit together.
```

Handoff possibility: soul and vintage sewing-supply research, unhurried breakfast, separate-duvet preference.
Review: brief answers support depth; the extra button question has plausible value but should not become a mandatory hobby interrogation.

## 052 — Efficient guest

```text
G: I travel constantly. Please keep this practical: work, early starts, quiet nights.
H: What time do your mornings start at the hotel?
G: Coffee at six, out at six forty-five.
H: How do you take your coffee?
G: Black. I do not eat breakfast.
H: What is the main thing that disturbs your sleep?
G: Connecting doors and corridor noise.
H: Anything else essential for the team?
G: No, that is everything.
H: Thank you. We can leave it there.
```

Handoff possibility: early black coffee and quiet-room requests, subject to feasibility; no breakfast preference invented.
Review: efficient because explicitly requested, not because the guest's sentences are short.

## 053 — Expansive storyteller

```text
G: My husband and I are finally coming back. Last time it rained every day, we argued about maps, found a wonderful café, and somehow had the best holiday.
H: What made that café such a good memory?
G: The owner talked to us for ages, and my husband still mentions the cherry cake. I mostly remember how welcome we felt.
H: Would you enjoy finding places with that same easy welcome this time?
G: Yes. We do not need the exact café. He likes old bookshops too, and I like gardens.
H: How much do you enjoy fitting into a day?
G: Very little. One outing, then we linger somewhere. We have spent enough holidays rushing.
H: Anything about food that the team should keep in mind?
G: He likes cake, I like savoury things. No allergies. We both drink tea, never coffee.
H: What helps you feel comfortable at night?
G: Darkness and separate blankets. We have perfected that over the years.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, I have probably told you plenty.
H: Thank you — it has been a pleasure hearing about that return visit.
```

Handoff possibility: warm personal service, cherry cake versus savoury tastes, gardens/bookshops, slow rhythm and bedding.
Review: selects a meaningful thread from a long answer; does not probe the argument or each anecdote.

## 054 — Companion changes

```text
G: The reservation says two adults, but my partner cannot come. My sister is coming instead.
H: Thank you for explaining. What would you and your sister enjoy during the stay?
G: Exploring together. First visit for her, second for me.
H: Is there something from your first visit you would like to share with her?
G: The riverside walk. She loves taking photographs.
H: What do you enjoy along the way?
G: Little shops and coffee stops.
H: Is there anything about the room setup the team should review with that change?
G: Twin beds would be preferable, if possible.
H: Any food preferences worth sharing?
G: We are both vegetarian. She dislikes eggs.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — the team will need to review the room request.
```

Handoff possibility: new companion context, first/return distinction, twin-bed request, vegetarian preferences.
Review: discovers a changed practical need without asserting the booking or bed allocation has been amended.

## 055 — Refusal of one subject

```text
G: A solo city break. I would rather not discuss sleep habits.
H: Of course. What are you looking forward to exploring?
G: Street photography. First time in this city.
H: What do you like photographing?
G: Everyday scenes. People working, small shops, early markets.
H: Do you like going out early for that?
G: Yes, then a long lunch and a quiet afternoon.
H: What would make lunch enjoyable for you?
G: Fish, vegetables, somewhere I can sit outside. No allergies.
H: How do you enjoy receiving local suggestions?
G: I like hearing people's own favourites, then deciding for myself.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy exploring with your camera.
```

Handoff possibility: early everyday-life photography, outdoor lunch and personal recommendations; sleep topic declined.
Review: refusal is local to one subject; no disguised return through pillows or blankets.

## 056 — Dislike versus allergy

```text
G: Here with my husband for food and wandering. Neither of us likes shellfish.
H: Is shellfish a dislike, or something you need to avoid for an allergy?
G: Just dislike. We both love ordinary fish.
H: What kind of meal do you most enjoy when travelling?
G: A lively little place where someone explains the dishes.
H: Have you visited before?
G: No. We are curious about local flavours.
H: Any particular flavours you enjoy or avoid?
G: Citrus and herbs are good. Very sweet sauces are not.
H: What is your usual rhythm for exploring?
G: Slow mornings, long lunch, perhaps a walk afterwards.
H: Anything important for your comfort at the hotel?
G: A neutral smell. Otherwise we are easy.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No.
H: Thank you — enjoy discovering the food together.
```

Handoff possibility: explanatory local dining with fish, citrus/herbs, no shellfish by taste; neutral fragrance preference.
Review: concise clarification is useful when dining is central; not every food dislike needs a separate medical follow-up.

## 057 — Child routine and adults

```text
G: A family holiday. Our daughter loves bedtime stories about dragons.
H: Does she have a favourite dragon story?
G: She makes them up herself. The dragon is afraid of mice.
H: Would she enjoy a little note about her dragon when you arrive?
G: Yes, that would make her laugh.
H: What are the adults looking forward to on this holiday?
G: My wife wants to try new food. I want time to draw.
H: What food does your wife enjoy discovering?
G: Soups and spicy things. Our daughter prefers plain noodles.
H: Any dietary needs in the family?
G: No allergies. I am vegetarian.
H: What makes the day's rhythm work for everyone?
G: Early outings, then a quiet hour before dinner. Bedtime is important.
H: Anything else you would like the team to know?
G: No, thank you.
H: Thank you — enjoy your family holiday.
```

Handoff possibility: non-food dragon note, distinct food preferences and quiet pre-dinner rhythm; drawing interest for the parent.
Review: adult drawing remains broad; one deeper question might have been more valuable than the final generic invitation. City familiarity is also unknown.

## 058 — Past milestone

```text
G: A break with my wife. I retired last year, but we already celebrated that.
H: What would you enjoy about this break now?
G: Time for small museums and lunches. No retirement fuss.
H: What kinds of museums interest you?
G: Local history, especially ordinary people's lives.
H: Does your wife enjoy the same things?
G: Yes, but she likes gardens more. We take turns choosing.
H: What makes a good lunch for you both?
G: Somewhere relaxed. Soup and something savoury, nothing elaborate.
H: Any food requirements?
G: None. We know the city and just want to take it slowly.
H: Anything for your comfort at the hotel?
G: Good light for reading. That is enough.
H: Thank you — enjoy taking your time together.
```

Handoff possibility: everyday-history museums, gardens and easy lunches; no present retirement celebration inferred.
Review: enough context is volunteered; unnecessary first-visit question avoided.

## 059 — Work habits and free evening

```text
G: Three days of meetings, but Tuesday evening is finally free.
H: What would you like that free evening to feel like?
G: A little human conversation. I eat alone so much when travelling.
H: What sort of place makes conversation easy for you?
G: A counter or small bar serving food. Nothing with loud music.
H: What time will you be free on Tuesday?
G: Six thirty. I would eat around eight.
H: What do you enjoy eating?
G: Grilled vegetables and fish. No allergies.
H: What helps your mornings run smoothly?
G: Espresso, toast, and out by eight. I sleep fine anywhere.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — I hope Tuesday gives you a welcome change of pace.
```

Handoff possibility: sociable counter dining without loud music on the specified evening; clear morning rhythm.
Review: desire for company is explicitly stated, not inferred from solo travel.

## 060 — Apparent contradiction

```text
G: I love flowers. But please keep the room free of them.
H: Of course. Are flowers something you enjoy more when you are out exploring?
G: Exactly. Gardens and flower markets, just not where I sleep.
H: What do you enjoy most about those places?
G: Sketching shapes and colours. I do not know the names.
H: Is this your first visit to the city?
G: Yes, travelling alone with my sketchbook.
H: Do you like to settle in and draw for a while?
G: Yes, ideally with somewhere to sit.
H: What do you enjoy between sketching stops?
G: Coffee and a savoury pastry. I do not eat meat.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No, thank you.
H: Thank you — enjoy the sketching.
```

Handoff possibility: seated garden/flower-market research and vegetarian savoury pauses; no flowers in the room.
Review: two context-dependent preferences coexist without forcing the guest to retract either one.
