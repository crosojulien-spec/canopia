# Batch 01 — cases 001–010

Writing guidance: Behavior 00. Offline, self-authored roleplays; no application or model API execution.

## 001 — Child birthday

```text
G: My wife and I are bringing our son for his birthday.
H: Lovely — have you planned anything for the occasion yet?
G: Nothing yet. He is obsessed with dinosaurs.
H: What does he enjoy most about them?
G: Drawing his own and giving them ridiculous names.
H: Does he have a favourite creation?
G: Toastosaurus. It breathes toast instead of fire.
H: Toastosaurus has breakfast covered. Would he enjoy a little birthday note with a nod to that?
G: Yes, privately. Singing in public would embarrass him.
H: What would you and your wife enjoy during the trip?
G: I like old streets. She loves food markets.
H: What helps him settle in somewhere new?
G: His blue rabbit comes everywhere. We are all easy sleepers.
H: Anything else you would like the team to understand?
G: No, that is everything.
H: Thank you — a quiet birthday with room for all three of you to enjoy the city.
```

Handoff possibility: private illustrated birthday reference; old streets and market research for the parents. Rabbit is existing personal property, not an item to buy.
Review: useful specificity and companion inclusion; first versus return visit remains unknown despite the city interest.

## 002 — Returning city couple

```text
G: We are coming back to Prague, my partner and me.
H: Is there a memory from the last visit you would like to revisit?
G: A tiny record shop, though I cannot remember its name.
H: What records were you hoping to find?
G: Old soul. My partner was more interested in the architecture outside.
H: What catches your partner's eye in a building?
G: Painted doorways and unusual windows.
H: How do you like to spend the morning before exploring?
G: Slowly. Strong coffee and something with lemon.
H: Are there any food restrictions for either of you?
G: None. I hate raisins, though.
H: What makes a hotel night comfortable for you both?
G: I get hot and they get cold. One blanket each helps.
H: Anything you would like to add before we finish?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy rediscovering the city together.
```

Handoff possibility: soul-record and architectural wandering research, lemon breakfast preference, separate bedding request.
Review: usable texture; no need to pressure the guest to identify the lost shop.

## 003 — Solo work evenings

```text
G: Alone for meetings. I would like the evenings to feel less like work.
H: What helps you switch off after a meeting day?
G: Sitting at a restaurant counter and listening to blues somewhere.
H: What would you enjoy eating?
G: Grilled fish, without heavy sauces.
H: Is there a blues musician you particularly enjoy?
G: Buddy Guy. But a friendly place matters more than a famous act.
H: How does a good work morning start for you?
G: A short run, double espresso, out by eight.
H: How long do you like to run?
G: Twenty minutes. An easy route would help.
H: Anything else you would like the team to know?
G: No, that covers it.
H: Thank you — I hope you find some good evenings between the meetings.
```

Handoff possibility: counter dining and blues research; easy run with time for coffee.
Review: premature closure; evening availability and any relevant comfort opening were left unexplored while the guest was participating.

## 004 — Tired couple

```text
G: We mostly need a break. We have been doing too much lately.
H: What makes a few days away feel restful for you?
G: No schedule. Reading by the water and long breakfasts.
H: What are you reading at the moment?
G: Me, detective stories. My husband reads history.
H: What do you both like at a long breakfast?
G: Eggs for him. Fruit and plain yoghurt for me.
H: Anything in a hotel room that tends to disturb your rest?
G: Strong perfume. We both hate it.
H: How do you like the team to offer help during a stay like this?
G: Happy to chat, just not be given a timetable.
H: Is there anything you would like to add?
G: No, thank you.
H: Thank you — time to read, linger and leave the days open.
```

Handoff possibility: unscented preparation, separate breakfast preferences, unhurried help.
Review: proportionate discovery; no reason to demand exact book titles or an occasion.

## 005 — Mother and adult daughter

```text
G: It is a weekend with my mother. She has always wanted to see the city.
H: What has she been most looking forward to?
G: The old buildings. I like stopping in little shops.
H: Does she enjoy hearing the stories behind the buildings?
G: Very much. But she cannot walk all day.
H: What pace usually works comfortably for her?
G: About twenty minutes, then a sit-down. Stairs are difficult.
H: What kind of stop would you both enjoy?
G: Tea and cake. Mum loves anything with cherries.
H: Any dietary needs to keep in mind?
G: None. I prefer chocolate to fruit cakes.
H: What helps you both settle in at the hotel?
G: Tea in the evening and enough light to read.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: That is all.
H: Thank you — a gentle pace with good places to pause.
```

Handoff possibility: seated breaks, avoid difficult stairs, individual cake tastes, reading light request.
Review: useful practical and personal discovery without asking for a diagnosis.

## 006 — First solo holiday

```text
G: First holiday on my own. I want to explore but not rush.
H: What do you most enjoy discovering when you have a day to yourself?
G: Small galleries and places to draw.
H: Do you like settling in to draw, or making quick sketches as you go?
G: Sitting for an hour with a coffee.
H: What usually catches your eye?
G: Everyday things. People waiting, shop windows.
H: How do you enjoy eating when travelling alone?
G: Somewhere informal where talking to the person beside me feels normal.
H: What would you enjoy ordering?
G: Vegetables, interesting spices. I am vegetarian.
H: Anything that helps you sleep well away from home?
G: Darkness. I bring my own mask.
H: Anything else you would like to share?
G: No, thank you.
H: Thank you — time for your sketchbook and some good conversations.
```

Handoff possibility: places to linger and sketch, sociable vegetarian dining research. No loneliness inference.
Review: good solo discovery; destination familiarity was not established.

## 007 — Friends with different tastes

```text
G: Three friends, a weekend together after a long time apart.
H: What would make the weekend feel like time well spent together?
G: Long meals and a little live music. Nothing organised.
H: What music do you enjoy?
G: I like jazz. Bea likes dancing. Chen mostly comes for the food.
H: What food does Chen get excited about?
G: Dumplings. Bea is vegetarian, and I eat anything.
H: What do you like doing between meals?
G: Wandering and talking. We do not need famous sights.
H: What makes the room comfortable for the three of you?
G: We have separate rooms, so our sleep habits do not need coordinating.
H: Of course. Anything else for the team?
G: No, we are easy.
H: Thank you — space to catch up, with food and music along the way.
```

Handoff possibility: informal shared meals with vegetarian options; music research should distinguish jazz and dancing.
Review: unsupported assumption of a shared room; accommodation configuration needed before a joint-comfort question.

## 008 — Short informative replies

```text
G: City break. Me and my brother.
H: Have you visited before?
G: First time.
H: What tends to draw you into a place?
G: Street art.
H: What do you like about it?
G: Big murals. He photographs them.
H: Do you make photographs together or enjoy different things along the way?
G: I draw. He shoots.
H: What is your usual rhythm for a day out?
G: Late start. Long lunch.
H: Any lunch preferences worth knowing?
G: Spicy food. No allergies.
H: Anything that helps you settle in after a day outside?
G: A hot shower. No scented room.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: Nope. Thanks.
H: Thank you — plenty of time for murals, lunch and your own ways of seeing the city.
```

Handoff possibility: mural exploration, sketching and photography, spicy lunch research, no added room scent.
Review: short replies remain informative; final sentence could be plainer.

## 009 — Private family reason

```text
G: I am coming for a family matter. I would rather not explain it.
H: Of course. Is there anything practical that would make the stay easier?
G: A quiet room, and no fuss when I arrive.
H: Is there a particular source of noise you would like the team to know about?
G: Lifts and corridors. I sleep lightly.
H: Would you like to leave it there?
G: Yes, please.
H: Thank you. We can finish here.
```

Handoff possibility: quiet-room location request, no conspicuous arrival attention, subject to the hotel's allocation.
Review: appropriate brevity and respect for the private reason; no inferred family event or diagnosis.

## 010 — Anniversary, no note

```text
G: Our anniversary, but please no little cards or decorations.
H: Understood. Have you planned anything you are looking forward to?
G: A dinner we booked ourselves. Otherwise just walking.
H: What kinds of places do you enjoy walking through together?
G: Gardens and quiet streets. We are here for the first time.
H: What makes a good pause on those walks?
G: Coffee and pastries. Almond ones for me, chocolate for my wife.
H: Any dietary constraints around those?
G: No. We just dislike big, formal lunches.
H: What helps you both feel comfortable at night?
G: Two separate duvets. We always travel like that.
H: Is there anything you would like to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — I hope you enjoy your anniversary at your own pace.
```

Handoff possibility: garden walks, separate pastry tastes and duvets; existing dinner is already arranged.
Review: note refusal respected; no need to invent an alternative surprise.
