# Batch 04 — cases 031–040

Writing guidance: Behavior 03. Self-authored offline roleplays.

## 031 — Child dislikes surprises

```text
G: Our son's birthday, but he hates surprises of any kind.
H: Have you planned something he is looking forward to?
G: A museum he chose. He likes old trains.
H: What interests him about them?
G: How they work. He draws wheels and engines constantly.
H: What do you enjoy doing together around a museum visit?
G: Lunch, then time for his sketchbook. He is happiest without rushing.
H: What lunch choices tend to work well?
G: Plain pasta for him. We adults like trying local food.
H: Any food restrictions?
G: No. He just dislikes mixed sauces.
H: Anything that makes unfamiliar rooms easier for him?
G: Knowing what to expect. Please do not add decorations.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy the museum together.
```

Handoff possibility: predictable room preparation and unhurried museum day; no birthday note or surprise proposed.
Review: dislike of surprises respected beyond the narrower issue of public attention. Destination familiarity remains unspecified.

## 032 — Attribution correction

```text
G: My partner likes jazz and I like photography. First visit here.
H: What do you enjoy photographing?
G: Sorry, I swapped us around. I like jazz; she takes the photographs.
H: What kind of jazz do you enjoy?
G: Piano trios, especially small rooms where you can really listen.
H: What catches her eye with a camera?
G: Reflections and shop windows. She likes going out early.
H: Do you join her early walks?
G: Sometimes. Mostly I meet her for breakfast afterwards.
H: What do you each enjoy for breakfast?
G: Coffee for both, savoury for me, fruit for her.
H: Any dislikes or food requirements?
G: None. We are flexible.
H: Anything that matters for your room comfort?
G: Darkness at night. Nothing else.
H: Anything to add or correct?
G: No, you have it now.
H: Thank you — enjoy your first visit.
```

Handoff possibility: piano jazz for the speaker, early reflection photography for the partner; distinct breakfast tastes.
Review: original attribution must be replaced, not accumulated as a second set of facts.

## 033 — Different sleep needs

```text
G: Coming back for a quiet weekend. Sharing a room, but we sleep very differently.
H: What tends to be the difference?
G: I get cold. My wife sleeps hot and wakes with the light.
H: How do you make that work at home?
G: Separate duvets and heavy curtains. It works well.
H: What are you looking forward to during the days?
G: Old bookshops and slow lunches. We know the city well.
H: What do you like finding in bookshops?
G: I collect maps. She reads crime novels.
H: What makes a lunch good for you both?
G: Simple fish, somewhere we can sit a while. No allergies.
H: Anything else worth sharing?
G: No, that is enough.
H: Thank you — I hope you find a good map or two.
```

Handoff possibility: separate duvets and darkness request; map and crime-book research with unhurried fish lunch.
Review: no need to demand exact degrees after a workable existing routine is explained.

## 034 — Solo birthday

```text
G: It is my birthday and I am treating myself to a weekend alone.
H: Have you planned something you are excited about?
G: A long lunch. After that, nothing at all.
H: What would make that lunch feel like a treat?
G: Sitting outside, good seafood and a glass of wine.
H: Anything you dislike or need to avoid?
G: No restrictions. I really dislike rushed service.
H: What do you enjoy when you have the afternoon to yourself?
G: Reading. A funny novel, nothing worthy.
H: Would you enjoy a quiet birthday acknowledgement, or prefer the occasion left to you?
G: Leave it to me, please.
H: Anything that would make the room comfortable?
G: No strong scent. That is everything.
H: Thank you — enjoy your birthday weekend.
```

Handoff possibility: unhurried outdoor seafood lunch research, low-fragrance room request; no birthday gesture.
Review: solo celebration is not loneliness or an automatic note opportunity.

## 035 — Three generations

```text
G: My parents, my wife and our daughter. First time here for all of us.
H: What are you most hoping to enjoy together?
G: A few discoveries, but my father needs a slower pace.
H: What pace feels comfortable for him?
G: Short walks with a seat every fifteen minutes. He likes old engineering.
H: What tends to interest your daughter?
G: Bridges and drawing boats. Mum loves gardens.
H: And what would you and your wife enjoy?
G: Food markets, especially tasting things we do not know.
H: Any food constraints in the group?
G: My wife is vegetarian. No allergies that I know of.
H: What helps the different generations enjoy the day together?
G: One outing, then time apart. We have three rooms.
H: Anything useful about settling in at the hotel?
G: I cannot answer for everyone. We can leave it there.
H: Of course. Thank you.
```

Handoff possibility: short accessible outings combining engineering, boats and gardens; vegetarian tasting options.
Review: inclusion risks becoming a roll call. Unknown allergies cannot be converted into verified absence.

## 036 — Temporary deadline

```text
G: I am here for work, and I only have a minute now.
H: What is the most useful thing for the team to know before you arrive?
G: I leave at six thirty each morning. Coffee matters more than breakfast.
H: How do you take it?
G: Black, strong. I need to get back to a call now.
H: Of course. Thank you — we can finish here.
```

Handoff possibility: early coffee preference and schedule for the team to check; no availability promised.
Review: a clear time boundary outweighs possible evening or comfort questions.

## 037 — Overwhelmed first visitor

```text
G: First visit. There is so much to do that I do not know where to start.
H: When you have enjoyed a city before, what kind of moment made it memorable?
G: Sitting somewhere with a view and having no reason to move.
H: What do you enjoy doing while you sit there?
G: Coffee, a book, watching people. I do not need a packed itinerary.
H: Are you travelling on your own?
G: With my husband. He is much the same, but likes taking photographs.
H: What kind of food do you both enjoy when you are out?
G: Simple local dishes. I cannot eat gluten.
H: Is that an allergy, intolerance, or another requirement the team should record?
G: Coeliac disease. I need restaurants to take cross-contact seriously.
H: Anything about the hotel that would help you unwind?
G: Quiet nights. Nothing elaborate.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, I feel less pressured already.
H: Thank you — there is room to enjoy the city slowly.
```

Handoff possibility: unhurried viewpoints and serious gluten/cross-contact requirement; no crowded itinerary.
Review: question about comparable enjoyable moments helps without asking for a ready-made plan.

## 038 — Return with novelty

```text
G: We know the city. This time we want something different from museums.
H: What are you curious to try instead?
G: Food and crafts. Watching people make things.
H: What kinds of making catch your interest?
G: Ceramics for me. My partner likes woodworking.
H: Would you enjoy watching, trying it yourselves, or either?
G: Watching, mostly. We do not need a class.
H: What is your usual pace for a day out?
G: One thing in the morning, long lunch, then see how we feel.
H: Any lunch preferences?
G: Vegetarian for both. Spicy is good.
H: Anything that would help you settle in afterwards?
G: Just a quiet room. We are easy sleepers.
H: Anything else?
G: No.
H: Thank you — enjoy seeing a different side of the city.
```

Handoff possibility: craft observation and spicy vegetarian lunch research; no inferred class purchase.
Review: distinguishes curiosity from willingness to participate or pay.

## 039 — Humour

```text
G: Romantic weekend. The only thing my husband loves more than me is a good sausage.
H: Sounds like dinner has serious competition. What do you actually enjoy eating together?
G: Ha! Small plates and seafood. No enormous meat platters.
H: Have you planned anything for the weekend?
G: Nothing. We know the city and like leaving things open.
H: What do you enjoy finding on an unplanned afternoon?
G: A little bar with live music. Soul, preferably.
H: Any food or drink boundaries to keep in mind?
G: No allergies. He does not drink alcohol.
H: What does he enjoy drinking instead?
G: Something sharp with lime, not too sweet.
H: Anything for your comfort at night?
G: No, nothing special. That is all.
H: Thank you — enjoy the weekend together.
```

Handoff possibility: shared seafood plates, soul music, lime-based non-alcoholic preference for husband.
Review: joke not captured as a literal sausage preference; host humour may still feel rehearsed and needs human review.

## 040 — Late comfort addition

```text
G: First visit with my sister, mainly galleries and coffee.
H: What art do you each enjoy?
G: Photography for her, textiles for me.
H: Do you like spending a long time in one place?
G: Yes, then a long coffee afterwards.
H: What do you like with the coffee?
G: Dark chocolate. No dietary restrictions.
H: Anything that helps you settle in at night?
G: We are both quite easy.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: Actually, separate blankets would help. She steals mine.
H: Blanket peace treaty, then — one each is your preference.
G: Exactly. Thanks, that is it.
H: Thank you — enjoy the galleries.
```

Handoff possibility: galleries by medium, long coffee pauses, dark chocolate and separate blanket request.
Review: no unnecessary clarification when the late addition already supplies the preference; stock duvet jokes should not become a template.
