# Batch 02 — cases 011–020

Writing guidance: Behavior 01. Self-authored offline roleplays.

## 011 — Birthday without a plan

```text
G: We are celebrating our daughter's birthday. No plans yet.
H: What is she enjoying most at the moment?
G: Making little comics about our dog.
H: What is the dog like in her comics?
G: A detective who keeps losing his glasses.
H: Would she enjoy a small birthday note with that detective in mind?
G: She would. She is shy, so nothing in front of people.
H: Is this your first visit to the city?
G: Yes. We would all like to explore a bit.
H: What do the adults enjoy discovering?
G: My husband likes old trams. I like food markets.
H: What makes a day out work well for your daughter?
G: A break to draw after lunch. Nothing too scheduled.
H: What helps everyone settle in for the night?
G: We are easy, but please no perfume in the room.
H: Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, thank you.
H: Thank you — a private birthday and time for her next detective story.
```

Handoff possibility: private comic reference, tram and market research, drawing pause, no added fragrance.
Review: coherent breadth; useful food interests but no specific flavours, which can remain unknown.

## 012 — Work plus leisure

```text
G: Work until Friday, then my partner joins me for the weekend.
H: What would you like the weekend to feel like after work?
G: Lazy. We have both visited before and want fewer sights this time.
H: Is there something from earlier visits you would like to return to?
G: Sitting by the river with a coffee.
H: During the workdays, what timing should the team understand?
G: Out at seven, back after eight. Breakfast needs to be simple.
H: What is a reliable quick breakfast for you?
G: Yoghurt and espresso. My partner likes a long breakfast once she arrives.
H: Anything different about how you like to sleep?
G: I sleep cool. She needs an extra blanket.
H: Any food requirements for either of you?
G: She avoids dairy. I do not.
H: Anything else for the team?
G: No, that is enough.
H: Thank you — early work mornings, then a slower weekend together.
```

Handoff possibility: date-dependent breakfast needs and extra blanket request; dairy avoidance belongs to the partner.
Review: early departure may need hotel feasibility checking; no breakfast availability promised.

## 013 — Separate interests

```text
G: First time here with my partner. I want museums; he wants markets.
H: What kinds of museums draw you in?
G: Design, especially everyday objects.
H: And what is he looking for in a market?
G: Food and people. He can talk to anyone.
H: Do you enjoy splitting up sometimes, or prefer finding things together?
G: A little time apart is good, then meeting for lunch.
H: What makes a good lunch for both of you?
G: Small local places. I am vegetarian; he eats everything.
H: How do you like to spend the evening back at the hotel?
G: Reading. Two chairs would be nicer than both sitting on the bed.
H: Anything else that would help the team understand your stay?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — separate discoveries with an easy lunch to meet over.
```

Handoff possibility: design and market research with a shared lunch; seating preference to check.
Review: practical comfort emerged naturally without a sleep checklist.

## 014 — Late stopover

```text
G: I arrive at midnight and leave at five. I just need sleep.
H: Is there anything specific that tends to disturb your sleep in hotels?
G: Corridor noise. I have my own eye mask.
H: Anything essential before you head out at five?
G: No breakfast, no wake-up call. I have everything covered.
H: Understood. We can leave it there.
G: Thanks.
H: Thank you for letting us know.
```

Handoff possibility: quiet-location request; no breakfast or wake-up service solicitation.
Review: appropriate short discovery. Do not expand merely to fill this workshop's topics.

## 015 — Unknown partner tastes

```text
G: A weekend with my boyfriend. I arranged it as a surprise.
H: What are you most looking forward to sharing with him?
G: Exploring somewhere new. Neither of us knows the city.
H: What does he usually enjoy discovering?
G: Restaurants, but I honestly cannot tell you his favourite food.
H: What kind of restaurant feels comfortable for you together?
G: Casual. We like sharing dishes and people-watching.
H: What would you enjoy outside mealtimes?
G: Bookshops. He is happy to wander.
H: What do you like to read?
G: Novels in translation.
H: Anything about settling in that you already know for both of you?
G: I like a firm pillow. I do not know about him.
H: Anything else you would like to share?
G: No.
H: Thank you — those details give the team a useful starting point.
```

Handoff possibility: casual shared dining and translated-literature bookshops; his food and pillow tastes remain unknown.
Review: birthday-style note is not obligatory for a surprise trip; no pressure to answer for the boyfriend.

## 016 — No pillow preference

```text
G: We are visiting friends here, and fitting in some walks.
H: Have you spent time in the city before?
G: Often. We mostly want to catch up with them.
H: What does a good catch-up usually involve?
G: A long dinner, then cards. We play rummy.
H: Any favourite food or drink for an evening like that?
G: Cheese and red wine. No restrictions.
H: What helps you both sleep comfortably away from home?
G: No pillow preference. Honestly, anything is fine.
H: What about temperature or blankets?
G: We really are not particular about any of it.
H: Fair enough. Anything else you would like to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — enjoy the reunion and the rummy.
```

Handoff possibility: unhurried reunion dining and a card-playing setting to consider.
Review: needless second comfort probe after broad indifference; the first answer should have ended that area.

## 017 — Hobby without a favourite

```text
G: I am coming alone to paint for a few days. First visit.
H: What do you like painting when you travel?
G: Light and colours. I do not know art terminology.
H: Is there a particular artist whose work you enjoy?
G: Not really. I just do it for myself.
H: When do you tend to go out with your paints?
G: Early, before people are around. Then a slow breakfast.
H: What do you enjoy at breakfast?
G: Tea and something savoury. I do not eat eggs.
H: Anything about the room that would make the stay easier?
G: A little space to let paintings dry, if possible.
H: Anything else you would like to share?
G: No, that is it.
H: Thank you — quiet mornings and room for your paintings.
```

Handoff possibility: quiet early painting locations, savoury egg-free breakfast preference, drying-space request.
Review: artist question adds burden after the guest disclaims terminology; lived practice was the stronger route.

## 018 — Bedding assumption trap

```text
G: My husband and I are visiting from Sweden. First time in the city.
H: What are you hoping to enjoy here?
G: Long walks, bakeries and doing very little else.
H: What is your bakery weakness?
G: Mine is cardamom buns. His is anything with chocolate.
H: What helps you both feel comfortable at night?
G: We have different ideas of a comfortable temperature.
H: Do you prefer separate duvets, or does sharing work for you?
G: Sharing, actually. Just an extra blanket for me.
H: Any dislikes or requirements around food?
G: No allergies. He hates coconut.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: Nothing, thanks.
H: Thank you — shared duvet, with an extra blanket for you.
```

Handoff possibility: distinct pastry tastes and bedding request; no nationality-based preference assigned.
Review: direct choice is useful here; open questions need not be an absolute rule.

## 019 — Privacy question

```text
G: Why do you want to know what we like?
H: It helps the hotel's team prepare this stay around you. Sharing is optional, and you can skip any question.
G: Okay. We are coming for our first holiday without the children.
H: What are you looking forward to having time for?
G: Long dinners and sleeping late.
H: What makes a dinner feel special to you two?
G: Good seafood, nothing formal. We like talking to the staff.
H: Any food restrictions or things you dislike?
G: No restrictions. My wife dislikes oysters, but I love them.
H: Anything that tends to interrupt a lie-in?
G: Bright light. We both wake up early if the room is bright.
H: Anything else you would like the team to know?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — unhurried dinners and darker mornings.
```

Handoff possibility: relaxed seafood dining with different oyster tastes; darkness request, friendly interaction.
Review: purpose question addressed without inventing retention rules; concern is not treated as withdrawal.

## 020 — Explicit withdrawal

```text
G: I do not want personalisation. Please stop asking and do not use my answers for that.
H: We will stop here. The team will receive a minimal record saying that personalisation is not wanted.
G: Thank you.
H: You are welcome.
```

Handoff boundary: no new personalisation proposals; minimal withdrawal record only, per current product policy.
Review: no discovery question after withdrawal and no invented deletion promise.
