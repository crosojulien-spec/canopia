# Batch 03 — cases 021–030

Writing guidance: Behavior 02. Self-authored offline roleplays.

## 021 — Celebration already arranged

```text
G: Our anniversary. We have booked dinner and do not need help arranging it.
H: What would you enjoy around the dinner during your stay?
G: Wandering. We know the city and want to revisit the river.
H: What draws you back there?
G: We sat there talking until midnight on our first trip.
H: Would a quiet little note marking that memory appeal to you?
G: Yes, something simple. No decorations.
H: Is there a feeling you would like it to express?
G: That we still make time for each other.
H: What makes you both comfortable when you return to the hotel?
G: Tea, a book and no music playing in the room.
H: Anything you would like to add or correct?
G: No, thank you.
H: Thank you — that memory gives the team something personal to consider.
```

Handoff possibility: private note based on time together; quiet tea-and-reading preference. Dinner already booked elsewhere.
Review: respects arranged plans; the note sentiment is sufficient, with no wording workshop.

## 022 — Spontaneous artist detail

```text
G: First visit with my sister. She wants food markets; I would happily listen to Nina Simone all day.
H: What do you enjoy about listening to her?
G: The voice and the quieter songs. I am not asking for a concert.
H: And what does your sister enjoy finding in markets?
G: Ingredients she has never cooked with. She loves asking stallholders questions.
H: Do you enjoy cooking together?
G: Yes, slowly, with music on. That is our Sunday ritual.
H: Any ingredients either of you needs to avoid?
G: No allergies. I do not like raw onion.
H: What helps you settle in after a day out?
G: A quiet room. We both sleep easily.
H: Anything else before we leave it there?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — markets and your Sunday cooking ritual give a lovely picture of the two of you.
```

Handoff possibility: culinary discovery linked to their shared ritual; musical preference is context, not a concert request.
Review: good connection between two people's interests. Final response still leans on an evaluative compliment.

## 023 — An unnamed memory

```text
G: We last visited years ago. There was a courtyard café we loved, but I cannot remember where.
H: What made it stay with you?
G: Shade, a fountain and nobody hurrying us.
H: Would you enjoy finding somewhere with that same feeling this time?
G: Exactly. It does not have to be the same café.
H: What do you like to spend that unhurried time doing?
G: Reading and people-watching. My husband writes postcards.
H: What do you usually order when you settle in?
G: Coffee for me, mint tea for him. Something with apricots if there is any.
H: Any food needs to keep in mind?
G: None. We are quite flexible.
H: Anything for your comfort at the hotel?
G: Good reading light. Everything else is easy.
H: Anything else you want to share?
G: No.
H: Thank you — somewhere shaded and unhurried matters more than recovering the exact address.
```

Handoff possibility: research by atmosphere, reading and postcards; specific drinks and apricot preference.
Review: useful precision without chasing an unavailable name.

## 024 — Brief replies, sociable guest

```text
G: Alone. City break.
H: Have you visited before?
G: No.
H: What are you curious to explore?
G: Food.
H: What do you enjoy about discovering food somewhere new?
G: Talking to people who make it.
H: Is there anything you particularly enjoy eating?
G: Dumplings. Spicy soups.
H: Anything you avoid?
G: No shellfish. Allergy.
H: What should the team understand about that allergy?
G: Cross-contact too. I discuss it with restaurants.
H: How do you like to start the day?
G: Slowly. Coffee, then out.
H: Anything that would help you feel comfortable at the hotel?
G: Friendly staff. I like a chat.
H: Anything else before we finish?
G: All good.
H: Thank you — the allergy and your enjoyment of talking about food are both important for the team.
```

Handoff possibility: culinary conversations with explicit shellfish/cross-contact constraints; warm staff interaction.
Review: short messages do not imply distant service; no allergy safety assurance given.

## 025 — Late food correction

```text
G: Here for a conference, leaving mornings at eight. I want quick breakfast ideas.
H: What works well for you before a conference day?
G: Fruit and yoghurt. No dairy, though.
H: Should the team understand that as needing a dairy-free yoghurt?
G: Sorry, I meant no milk in coffee. Normal yoghurt is fine.
H: How do you take your coffee?
G: Black espresso. Then I am out the door.
H: Is there anything you enjoy doing when the conference finishes?
G: A walk, if it ends before seven. I do not know the schedule yet.
H: What kind of walk helps you unwind?
G: Somewhere green, not another crowded place.
H: Anything that helps you sleep after those days?
G: Cool air, without a fan blowing at my face.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — normal yoghurt is fine; the coffee is black.
```

Handoff possibility: quick breakfast preference, conditional green walk, cool room without direct airflow request.
Review: correction replaces an earlier restriction; exact evening availability appropriately remains unknown.

## 026 — Colleague pair

```text
G: My colleague and I are attending a trade fair. Separate rooms.
H: What would help make the fair days easier for you?
G: Early coffee, and a simple dinner afterwards.
H: What time do you need to leave in the morning?
G: Seven. He starts later; we are not always together.
H: What do you personally enjoy for that early start?
G: Espresso and toast. I do not know his routine.
H: What does a simple dinner mean for you?
G: Somewhere relaxed, a bowl of noodles, back early.
H: Any ingredients you avoid?
G: Peanuts. It is an allergy, and I will explain it directly to the restaurant.
H: Anything else that matters for your stay?
G: Just quiet at night. That is all.
H: Thank you — those are your preferences; your colleague's remain separate.
```

Handoff possibility: early individual breakfast and relaxed dinner research with allergy warning; colleague preferences unknown.
Review: no romantic or shared-schedule assumption. Clinical follow-up was not forced after a clear boundary.

## 027 — No gym confirmation

```text
G: I run every morning when I travel. Work trip this time.
H: What kind of run fits around your workday?
G: Five easy kilometres before breakfast. Outside, preferably.
H: When do you need to be ready to leave?
G: Eight thirty. I usually run at six thirty.
H: What do you like after the run?
G: Strong coffee and eggs. Nothing sweet.
H: What helps you unwind after work?
G: Reading somewhere comfortable. I do not need nightlife.
H: What do you enjoy reading?
G: History. I have brought a book already.
H: Anything useful to know about your room comfort?
G: A chair and decent light matter more than pillows.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No.
H: Thank you — an easy outdoor run and a comfortable reading spot.
```

Handoff possibility: outdoor running-route research, savoury breakfast and reading setup request.
Review: no gym assumption or unnecessary second book recommendation.

## 028 — Room request interrupts discovery

```text
G: First city visit with my wife. Can we have a high floor?
H: I can record that as a request for the team to check. Is it the view or being away from noise that matters most?
G: Noise. I am a light sleeper.
H: What are you both hoping to enjoy during the visit?
G: Architecture and long lunches.
H: What kinds of buildings catch your attention?
G: Old theatres. My wife likes the decorated interiors.
H: What would make a long lunch good for you two?
G: Somewhere lively, with local dishes. We like talking to waiters.
H: Any food constraints?
G: None, just no liver for me.
H: Anything else for the team?
G: No, thanks.
H: Thank you — quiet for sleep, lively conversation over lunch.
```

Handoff possibility: quiet-location request and architecture/lunch research. High floor is a request, not an allocation.
Review: handles the direct request without losing discovery; quiet room does not become discreet service.

## 029 — Evening availability

```text
G: Solo for work. I would love a small live-music place one evening.
H: What music would make it a good evening for you?
G: Acoustic folk. Somewhere I can sit rather than stand in a crowd.
H: What time are you usually free after work?
G: About eight, though Thursday could run late.
H: Would you normally eat before going out?
G: Yes, a quick vegetarian meal.
H: Any particular food dislikes?
G: Mushrooms. No allergies.
H: What makes your work mornings manageable?
G: Tea and a quiet breakfast. I leave around nine.
H: Anything about sleep or settling in worth sharing?
G: No special preferences.
H: Anything else to add?
G: No.
H: Thank you — seated folk music after dinner, with Thursday left flexible.
```

Handoff possibility: seated acoustic venue research compatible with availability; vegetarian meal without mushrooms.
Review: central timing clarified before closure; appropriate end of comfort branch.

## 030 — Guest leaves mid-topic

```text
G: We are coming with our daughter. First family holiday in ages.
H: What are you most looking forward to doing together?
G: Swimming and taking it slowly. She loves drawing animals.
H: What animals does she enjoy drawing?
G: Foxes. Sorry, I need to go now.
H: Of course — thank you for sharing that. We can finish here.
```

Handoff possibility: slow family rhythm and fox-drawing interest; no note consent or age inferred.
Review: normal completion, not withdrawal; open questions do not justify continuing after an explicit end.
